60 mm vs 80 mm vs 100 mm paver blocks

Use 60 mm paver blocks for footpaths, gardens, terraces and home driveways with light car use; 80 mm for daily cars, SUVs, colony roads and car parks; and 100 mm for buses, trucks, petrol pumps, factory yards and forklifts. The stone base under the block matters as much as its thickness.

Thickness at a glance

ThicknessUse it forStrength gradeStone sub-baseDig depth (approx.)

100 mmBuses, trucks, petrol pumps, factory roadsM40–M50250 mmabout 380 mm

  1. Why thicker blocks carry more

    A thicker block is stiffer and locks deeper into its neighbours, so a wheel load spreads over a wider area of base instead of pushing single blocks down. That is why ruts show first on thin blocks under heavy wheels.

  2. The base matters as much

    An 80 mm block on a poor base sinks sooner than a 60 mm block on a well-compacted one. Build the stone sub-base in compacted layers and fix kerbs at the edges; a thicker block will not rescue a weak base.

  3. When to step up a size

    Go one thickness up if delivery trucks, water tankers or construction vehicles will use the floor even now and then, or where cars turn sharply in one spot, such as at a gate.

  4. Which shapes come in which thickness

    Zig-zag, I-shape and rectangular blocks come in 60, 80 and 100 mm. Square, Combo 5, diamond and cosmic blocks come in 60 and 80 mm. Decorative finishes such as slate, speckled and hexagon tiles are made for foot traffic.

Questions

Is 60 mm paver block enough for cars?

For a home driveway with light, slow car use on a good base, yes. For daily cars, SUVs or colony roads, use 80 mm.

Which thickness for a petrol pump or truck yard?

100 mm, grade M40–M50, on a 250 mm compacted stone base. Use M50 on a 300 mm base where forklifts or containers run.
