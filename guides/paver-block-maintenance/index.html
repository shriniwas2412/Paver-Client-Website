Paver block care and maintenance

Paver blocks need little care: sweep regularly, wash once or twice a year, top up joint sand when it washes out, and lift and re-lay any patch that sinks. Unlike a concrete slab, a paver floor can always be repaired block by block.

  1. Regular cleaning

    Sweep weekly and wash with water and a stiff broom. A pressure washer is fine on a low setting held at an angle, then refill any joint sand it removes.

  2. Oil and tyre stains

    Soak fresh oil with sawdust or cement powder, then scrub with detergent and warm water. Older stains respond to a concrete degreaser. Avoid acid cleaners, which etch the surface.

  3. Weeds and moss

    Pull weeds and refill the joint with dry sand: full joints leave weeds no room. In shady damp spots, scrub moss off with a mild bleach solution and rinse well.

  4. Joint sand

    Check the joints after every monsoon and brush in fresh dry sand where it has washed out. Full joints keep the blocks locked together.

  5. Sunken or broken blocks

    Lift the blocks in the low area, add and level sand underneath, and re-lay the same blocks. Replace a broken block with one from the same colour batch.

  6. Sealing (optional)

    A breathable concrete sealer deepens the colour and resists stains. Apply it on clean, dry paving, and re-apply every two to three years.
