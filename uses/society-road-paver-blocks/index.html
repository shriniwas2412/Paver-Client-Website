Paver blocks for society and colony roads

For colony and society internal roads, use 80 mm I-shape (Behaton) or zig-zag interlocking blocks on a 200–250 mm compacted stone base, with kerb stones on both edges. Footpaths beside the road can be 60 mm. Builders can order phase by phase, with colour matched across batches.

Blocks we suggest

  • I-Shape (Behaton / Dumbbell) Paver Blocks
    80 mm. Locks on four sides and spreads wheel loads along the road.
  • Zig-Zag (Uni) Interlocking Paver Blocks
    80 mm. The other favourite for colony roads; strong under braking.
  • Rectangular Paver Blocks
    60 mm for footpaths, laid in running bond.
  • Concrete Kerb Stones
    Kerb stones on both edges of every road; curved kerbs at corners.

Kerbs first

Set kerb stones on a concrete bed along both edges before any block is laid. Without them the blocks slowly spread sideways under wheels and the road opens up at the edges.

Bands at gates and junctions

A band of red or yellow blocks across the road at gates, speed breakers and junctions slows traffic and marks crossings without paint.

Services under the road

Water, sewer and cable lines under a paver road can be reached by lifting the blocks and laying the same ones back. With a concrete road the cut stays visible for good.

Questions

Which paver block is best for a society road?

80 mm I-shape (Behaton) or zig-zag (uni) interlocking blocks. Both lock on all sides and spread car loads. Use 100 mm where buses, school vans or construction trucks run daily.

Why do I need kerb stones?

Kerbs hold the edge of the paving. Without them the outer blocks move outwards under wheels and the joints open up across the road.

Can we get the whole colony in one shade?

Yes. Tell us the total quantity at the start and we plan production so every phase comes from matched batches.

Do you give a supply plan for builders?

Yes. Send the road and footpath areas and your phase dates, and our team sends a supply plan with block counts, kerb lengths and delivery dates.
