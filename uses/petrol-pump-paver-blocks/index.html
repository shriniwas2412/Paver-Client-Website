Paver blocks for petrol pumps

Petrol pump forecourts carry tankers, trucks and braking cars, so use 100 mm zig-zag (uni) or I-shape (Behaton) interlocking blocks, grade M40–M50, on a 250 mm compacted stone base, with kerb stones round the islands and edges. Oil drips can be cleaned off, and a stained or damaged block can be lifted and swapped.

The tanker bay

The spot where the tanker stands to unload takes the heaviest load on the site. Use 100 mm blocks there and give it the full stone base, even if the car lanes are lighter.

Oil and fuel stains

Soak fresh spills with sawdust or cement powder, then scrub with detergent and warm water. A block that stays badly stained can be lifted and replaced with one from the same batch.

Keep the pump open

Paving can go in section by section. Each section takes traffic as soon as it is compacted and the joints are filled, so part of the forecourt can stay open while the rest is laid.

Questions

Which paver thickness for a petrol pump?

100 mm, grade M40–M50, on a 250 mm compacted stone base. Tankers and trucks turn and brake on a forecourt every day, so thinner blocks wear and rut.

Will diesel and oil damage paver blocks?

Spills stain concrete but do not weaken a well-made block. Clean fresh spills quickly; a breathable concrete sealer makes cleaning easier.

Can the forecourt be paved without closing the pump?

Yes. Lay it section by section; each section can be used once it is compacted and the joints are filled.

How many blocks for a 10,000 sq ft forecourt?

About 41,000 zig-zag blocks or about 37,800 I-shape blocks, including 5% extra for cutting. Send your drawing and we will confirm the count and kerb length.
